Q: What's diarrhea, and what should I do first?
A: Diarrhea is an intestinal infection from bacteria/viruses that messes up water absorption in your gut, causing loose stools, cramps, and dehydration. It works by inflaming the gut lining, forcing out fluids rapidly. If untreated, it leads to severe weakness, organ failure, or even death from fluid loss.

Q: Tell me about malaria.
A: Malaria comes from Plasmodium parasites spread by mosquitoes, infecting your red blood cells and causing chills and cyclic fevers. The parasite multiplies in your liver then bursts blood cells, releasing toxins. Untreated, it causes anemia, brain damage, or fatal organ failure. 

Natural first aid: Brew tulsi or ginger tea to ease fever, stay super hydrated, and use nets to block mosquitoes.

Q: How does dengue fever work?
A: It's a virus from Aedes mosquitoes causing high fever, killer joint pain, and rashes. The virus damages blood vessels and drops platelet counts, leading to leaks. Without treatment, you risk internal bleeding, shock, or death. 

Natural first aid: Drink coconut water or fresh papaya leaf juice to boost hydration and platelets; rest in a cool spot.

Q: What should I do if someone is choking?
A: Choking happens when an object blocks the airway. If they can cough, encourage it. If they can't breathe/speak, perform the Heimlich maneuver (abdominal thrusts). Stand behind them, wrap arms around waist, make a fist above navel, and pull inward/upward.

Natural first aid: Ensure airway is clear once object is out; sip warm water to soothe throat.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How to recognize a heart attack and what to do?
A: Signs include crushing chest pain, pain in left arm/jaw, and shortness of breath. It's a blockage of blood flow to the heart muscle. Untreated: permanent heart damage or death.

Natural first aid: Have the person sit and rest to reduce heart load. If they have prescribed aspirin, they can chew it (under medical advice). Call emergency services immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is the first aid for a snake bite?
A: Snake venom can affect the nervous system or blood. Keep the victim calm and still to slow venom spread. Do NOT cut the wound or try to suck out venom. 

Natural first aid: Wash the area with soap/water, keep the bitten limb below heart level, and get to a hospital with anti-venom immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How to handle a possible bone fracture?
A: Symptoms include deformity, inability to move the limb, and intense pain. Untreated: improper healing or nerve damage.

Natural first aid: Immobilize the area using a splint (stick/board) and bandage. Apply ice to reduce swelling (not directly on skin). Do not try to realign the bone.

Q: What is constipation?

Q: What is acid reflux (GERD)?

Q: What are hemorrhoids?

Q: What is a thyroid storm?

Q: What is gout?

Q: What is anemia?

Neurological Emergencies
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is a stroke?
A: A blocked or burst blood vessel in the brain depriving neurons of oxygen, causing sudden face drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech. Remember: F.A.S.T. (Face, Arms, Speech, Time). Untreated: permanent disability or death. Natural first aid: Keep the person calm and still; do NOT give food or water (swallowing reflex may be impaired) — call emergency services immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a seizure?
A: Abnormal electrical brain activity causing uncontrolled body shaking, blank staring, or loss of consciousness. Untreated repetitive seizures damage the brain. Natural first aid: Clear the area of hard objects, gently turn them on their side, do NOT restrain or put anything in their mouth, time the seizure; call emergency if it lasts over 5 minutes.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a concussion?
A: A brain injury from a blow to the head disrupting normal brain function, causing headache, confusion, dizziness, and memory gaps. Untreated: second-impact syndrome. Natural first aid: Physical and mental rest (no screens); apply ice pack to head for 20 minutes; monitor for worsening symptoms; see a doctor even if symptoms seem mild.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a migraine?
A: Intense throbbing headache (often one-sided) triggered by hormones, stress, or light, accompanied by nausea and light sensitivity. Caused by changes in brain chemistry and blood vessel dilation. Natural first aid: Rest in a quiet, dark room; apply lavender or peppermint oil to temples; cold or warm compress on the neck; drink water.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is vertigo?
A: A spinning sensation caused by inner ear crystal displacement (BPPV) or inner ear infection, causing dizziness and nausea with movement. Natural first aid: Sit or lie down slowly; try the Epley maneuver (head repositioning exercises); avoid sudden head movements; ginger tea helps nausea.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is fainting (syncope)?
A: Brief loss of consciousness from reduced blood flow to the brain due to low blood pressure, dehydration, or emotional shock. Natural first aid: Lay the person flat and elevate their legs above heart level; loosen tight clothing; if unconscious over 1 minute, call emergency services.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a panic attack?
A: A sudden episode of intense fear triggering physical symptoms — racing heart, chest pain, shortness of breath — despite no real danger. Caused by adrenaline surge. Natural first aid: Box breathing (inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s, hold 4s); ground yourself with 5-4-3-2-1 senses technique; splash cold water on your face.

A: Inflammation of a tendon from repetitive motion (common in wrists, elbows, and knees of frequent typists and athletes), causing sharp pain with movement. Q: What is conjunctivitis (pink eye)?

Q: What do I do if something is in my eye?
A: A foreign object (dust, eyelash) on the eye's surface causes tearing and pain. Do NOT rub. Natural first aid: Blink rapidly to trigger tears; flush eye with clean water from inner to outer corner; pull upper eyelid over lower to sweep the object out; if stuck after flushing, see a doctor.

Q: What causes sudden hearing loss?

Q: What is a stye (hordeolum)?
A: A bacterial infection of the eyelid oil gland forming a painful red lump at the eyelid edge. Natural first aid: Apply a warm wet cloth compress for 10–15 minutes 3–4 times a day to bring it to a head; never squeeze it; it usually resolves within 1–2 weeks.

Q: How do I treat a deep cut (laceration)?

Q: What is cellulitis?
A: A bacterial skin infection spreading under the skin, causing red, warm, swollen, and painful skin (usually on legs). If a red line tracks upward, it signals septicemia — a medical emergency. Natural first aid: Elevate the limb, apply clean bandage; requires antibiotics — see a doctor.

Q: What is an insect sting reaction?
A: Bee/wasp venom triggers local pain, redness, and swelling; rarely triggers anaphylaxis. Natural first aid: Remove the stinger by scraping sideways (not pinching); apply ice pack for 20 minutes; soothing paste of baking soda + water or raw honey reduces sting pain.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What are hives (urticaria)?
A: Raised, itchy welts on skin from allergic reactions to food, medication, or stress. Usually resolve within 24 hours. Natural first aid: Cool shower or cold compress; apply aloe vera gel; take an antihistamine if available; avoid the trigger.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is eczema?
A: Chronic inflammatory skin condition causing dry, itchy, and cracked skin triggered by allergens or irritants. Natural first aid: Moisturize with pure coconut oil or unscented shea butter immediately after bathing; use lukewarm (not hot) water; avoid harsh soaps and synthetic fabrics.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a blister?
A: Fluid-filled skin pocket formed by friction, heat, or chemical irritation as a protective response. Natural first aid: Do NOT pop it if intact; cover with a bandage to reduce friction; if it bursts, clean with soap and water and apply aloe vera; keep dry.

Q: What is frostbite?
A: Freezing of skin and tissues (ears, fingers, toes) from prolonged cold exposure causing numbness, white/gray skin, and waxy texture. Untreated: tissue death and amputation. Natural first aid: Move to warm area; rewarm in warm water (37–40°C) — NOT hot; do NOT rub or walk on frostbitten tissue; seek emergency care.

Cardiovascular Issues
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is hypertension (high blood pressure)?
A: Chronic elevated force of blood against artery walls, often symptomless, but damaging to heart, kidneys, and brain over time. Natural first aid (acute spike): Sit calmly, practice slow deep breathing for 5–10 minutes, drink a small amount of water; long-term: reduce salt, exercise, and manage stress.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is cardiac arrest vs. heart attack?
A: A heart attack is a circulation problem (blocked artery); cardiac arrest is an electrical problem (heart stops beating). In cardiac arrest, the person is unconscious and not breathing. Natural first aid: Begin CPR immediately (30 chest compressions + 2 rescue breaths); use AED if available; call emergency services.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How do I perform CPR?
A: CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) manually pumps blood when the heart stops. Natural first aid: Place heel of hand on the center of the chest; push hard and fast (100–120 compressions/min, about 5–6 cm deep); allow full chest recoil; give 2 rescue breaths every 30 compressions; continue until help arrives.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is angina?
A: Chest tightness or pressure from reduced blood flow to the heart during exertion, easing with rest. It's a warning sign of coronary artery disease. Natural first aid: Rest immediately; sit or lie down; if prescribed, use nitroglycerine spray; if pain lasts over 15 minutes or doesn't ease — treat as a heart attack.

Bites & Toxins
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What do I do about a jellyfish sting?
A: Jellyfish tentacles inject venom on contact, causing burning pain and redness. Natural first aid: Rinse with seawater (NOT fresh water, which activates more nematocysts); remove visible tentacles with a card (never bare hands); soak in hot water (as hot as tolerable) for 20 minutes to neutralize venom.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What about a dog bite?
A: Dog bites carry rabies and bacterial infection risks. Natural first aid: Wash wound vigorously with soap and running water for 5–10 minutes; apply antiseptic (neem or diluted turmeric paste); cover with clean bandage; seek medical care for rabies vaccination within 24 hours.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is food or plant poisoning?
A: Ingesting toxic plants or mushrooms causes nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and in severe cases, organ failure. Natural first aid: Do NOT induce vomiting unless instructed by poison control; keep the person awake; bring a sample of what was eaten to the hospital — call poison control or emergency immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is carbon monoxide poisoning?
A: Colorless, odorless CO gas from faulty heaters/generators displaces oxygen in the blood causing headache, confusion, and loss of consciousness. Natural first aid: Get everyone outside to fresh air immediately; do NOT re-enter; call emergency services; perform CPR if unconscious.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What if someone swallows a chemical?
A: Household chemicals (bleach, detergents) burn the throat and esophagus. Do NOT induce vomiting (chemicals burn again on the way up). Natural first aid: Rinse mouth with water; if conscious, give small sips of water or milk to dilute; call poison control or emergency services immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is croup in children?
A: A viral airway inflammation in young children causing a distinctive barking cough and stridor (high-pitched breathing). Natural first aid: Take the child into cool night air or sit in a steamy bathroom for 10–15 minutes; keep the child calm (crying worsens airway tightness); see a doctor.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What if a child swallows a small object?
A: Small objects usually pass harmlessly, but button batteries, magnets, and sharp objects are dangerous. Natural first aid: If the child is breathing normally, do NOT try to remove it yourself; go to emergency care immediately for X-ray; watch for drooling, pain, or difficulty swallowing.

════════════════════════════════════
Obstetric & Reproductive
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What are signs of a miscarriage in early pregnancy?
A: Heavy vaginal bleeding, cramping, and tissue passing in the first trimester may signal miscarriage. Natural first aid: Rest, stay calm, use a sanitary pad (not tampon); do NOT take NSAIDs; save any tissue in a clean container for the doctor; go to the hospital immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is preeclampsia?
A: A dangerous pregnancy complication involving high blood pressure and protein in urine after 20 weeks, causing headache, swollen hands/face, and vision changes. Natural first aid: Lie on your left side to improve blood flow to the baby; stay calm — this is a medical emergency; go to hospital immediately.

════════════════════════════════════
Environmental Emergencies
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is drowning/near drowning?
A: Water entering the lungs blocks oxygen exchange, causing loss of consciousness and cardiac arrest. Natural first aid: Remove the person from water; if not breathing, begin CPR immediately; even if they seem fine after, all near-drowning victims need hospital evaluation (secondary drowning risk within 24 hours).

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is altitude sickness?
A: Ascending too fast to high altitudes causes headache, dizziness, nausea, and breathlessness from reduced oxygen. Untreated: life-threatening pulmonary/cerebral edema. Natural first aid: Stop ascending, rest for 24 hours; drink plenty of water; ginger tea for nausea; if symptoms worsen, descend immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is heat exhaustion?
A: Heavy sweating, cold/pale/clammy skin, and weakness from heat exposure — a step before dangerous heatstroke. Natural first aid: Move to a cool area, remove excess clothing, apply cold damp cloths, sip water/electrolyte drinks slowly; if confusion or hot dry skin develops, treat as heatstroke.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is hypothermia?
A: Core body temperature dropping dangerously low from cold exposure, causing shivering, confusion, slurred speech, and drowsiness. Untreated: heart failure. Natural first aid: Move to warmth; remove wet clothing; wrap in blankets (warm the torso first, not extremities); give warm sweet drinks if conscious; call emergency services.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is sunstroke vs. sunburn?
A: Sunburn is UV damage to skin cells (red, painful skin); sunstroke is systemic overheating with confusion. Natural first aid for sunburn: Cool shower, apply aloe vera gel, drink water; do NOT use butter or ice directly. For sunstroke: shade, cool water on body, electrolyte drinks — seek emergency care.

────────────────────────────────────

════════════════════════════════════
Dental & Mouth Emergencies
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is a toothache?
A: Pulp nerve irritation from decay, abscess, or fracture causing throbbing pain, sensitivity to hot/cold, and swelling. Natural first aid: Rinse with warm salt water; press a clove or dab clove oil on the area (eugenol numbs pain naturally); apply cold compress outside the jaw; see a dentist within 1–2 days.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What if a tooth is knocked out?
A: Trauma can dislodge a permanent tooth — act within 30 minutes for best chance of saving it. Natural first aid: Pick up by the crown (not root); rinse gently with milk or saline (not water); place it back in the socket or keep in cold milk; get to a dentist within 30 minutes.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a mouth ulcer (canker sore)?
A: Small painful lesions on the mouth's inner lining from stress, nutritional deficiency, or injury. Natural first aid: Apply honey or coconut oil directly; rinse with diluted hydrogen peroxide or salt water; eat soft cool foods; usually heals in 7–10 days.

Kidney & Urinary
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is kidney stone pain?
A: Mineral deposits in the kidney causing waves of excruciating flank pain radiating to the groin, bloody urine, and nausea. Natural first aid: Drink large amounts of water to help flush the stone; apply warm compress to the lower back; lemon juice in water (citrate may help dissolve small stones); seek medical evaluation.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is urine retention?
A: Inability to empty the bladder (from prostate enlargement, nerve damage, or obstruction) causing painful lower abdominal distension. Natural first aid: Warm compress on lower abdomen; try urinating in a warm bath; this is urgent — if no relief within an hour, go to emergency care for catheterization.

Blood & Bleeding
════════════════════════════════════

Q: How do I stop severe bleeding?
A: Major vessel damage causes rapid blood loss. Apply firm, direct pressure with clean cloth for 15+ minutes without lifting to check. Natural first aid: Do NOT remove embedded objects; if bleeding doesn't slow, pack the wound tightly and maintain pressure; if on a limb, a tourniquet 5–7 cm above the wound is a last resort — mark the time applied.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is internal bleeding?
A: Bleeding inside body cavities from trauma or ruptured organs, causing bruising, abdominal rigidity, dizziness, and shock without visible blood. Natural first aid: Lay the person flat with legs elevated (shock position); keep them warm; do NOT give food/water — call emergency services immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is shock?
A: A life-threatening state of critically reduced blood/oxygen flow to organs, causing pale clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, and confusion. Natural first aid: Lay the person flat, elevate legs 30 cm unless head/spinal injury is suspected; keep warm; loosen tight clothing; call emergency services; do NOT give anything by mouth.

════════════════════════════════════
Miscellaneous Medical Situations
════════════════════════════════════

Q: What is sunstroke in elderly people?
A: Older adults lose the ability to regulate temperature efficiently, making them highly vulnerable to fatal heat overload with subtle early symptoms. Natural first aid: Move to cool environment, apply ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, offer chilled fluids if fully conscious; seek emergency care immediately as their condition can deteriorate fast.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is insomnia and its immediate impact?
A: Chronic inability to fall or stay asleep caused by stress, caffeine, or anxiety, impairing memory, immune function, and mental health. Natural first aid: Drink warm chamomile or ashwagandha tea 30 minutes before bed; practice 4-7-8 breathing; use dim lighting; avoid screens 1 hour before sleep.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is an eye chemical splash?
A: Chemicals (acids, alkalis) splashing into the eye cause rapid corneal damage. Every second counts. Natural first aid: Immediately flush the eye with clean running water for 15–20 minutes continuously, holding eyelids open; remove contact lenses during flushing; go to emergency immediately even if pain subsides.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is numbness or tingling in hands/feet?
A: Paresthesia from nerve compression (sitting position, carpal tunnel), vitamin B12 deficiency, or poor circulation. Natural first aid: Shake out the limb and change position; massage the area; eat B12-rich foods (eggs, dairy, or supplements); if persistent without clear cause — see a doctor to rule out diabetes or neurological issues.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is hyperventilation syndrome?
A: Anxiety-driven over-breathing blows off too much CO₂, causing muscle spasms, tingling, and feeling of suffocation — ironically worsening panic. Natural first aid: Reassure the person they are not dying; coach them to breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6 counts; breathe through the nose; sitting cross-legged and grounding helps.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How do I handle an unconscious person?
A: Loss of consciousness can result from fainting, poisoning, head trauma, or cardiac arrest. Natural first aid: Check responsiveness (shout and tap shoulders); open airway (head tilt, chin lift); check for normal breathing for 10 seconds; if breathing — recovery position (on their side); if not breathing — begin CPR; call emergency services.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is rhabdomyolysis?
A: Severe muscle breakdown from extreme exercise, crush injury, or heat releasing myoglobin into blood, which can clog and destroy the kidneys. Warning sign: dark brown "cola-colored" urine after intense activity. Natural first aid: Drink large amounts of water immediately; rest; go to emergency care — IV fluids are needed urgently.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is an allergic reaction to medication?
A: Drug hypersensitivity causes hives, facial swelling, or breathing difficulty within hours of taking medicine. Natural first aid: Stop the medication immediately; if mild (hives only), antihistamines help; apply cool compress; if throat swelling or breathing difficulty — treat as anaphylaxis and call emergency services.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is a panic-induced hyperglycemia?
A: Acute stress releases cortisol and adrenaline, spiking blood sugar even in non-diabetics, causing headache and foggy thinking. Natural first aid: Sit and breathe slowly; drink water; do light stretching; practice mindfulness; if diabetic, test blood sugar and adjust medication plan per doctor's guidance.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is pericarditis?
A: Inflammation of the sac surrounding the heart (often viral) causing sharp chest pain that worsens lying down and improves leaning forward. Untreated: fluid buildup compressing the heart. Natural first aid: Sit upright or lean forward; apply cool compress; this requires urgent medical evaluation — seek emergency care.

FIRST AID KNOWLEDGE BASE — ENGLISH
SECTION 1: CORE PRINCIPLES

Q1: What is first aid?
A: Immediate help given to a sick or injured person before professional medical help arrives. Goals: preserve life, prevent worsening, promote recovery.

Q2: What is CHECK-CALL-CARE?
A: CHECK the scene and victim. CALL emergency services. CARE for the person based on their condition.

Q3: What is DR ABC?
A: D - Danger. R - Response. A - Airway. B - Breathing. C - Circulation. Check each in order before treating.

Q4: What is the recovery position?
A: Roll an unconscious but breathing person onto their side, tilt head back slightly. Prevents choking on vomit.

Q5: What personal protection should a first aider use?
A: Wear gloves. Use a face shield for rescue breaths. Wash hands before and after. Never put yourself in danger.

SECTION 2: CARDIAC EMERGENCIES

Q6: How do you perform CPR on an adult?
A: Place both hands on center of chest. Push hard and fast, at least 5 cm deep, 100-120 times per minute. After 30 compressions give 2 rescue breaths. Repeat until help arrives.

Q7: How do you use an AED?
A: Turn it on. Attach pads to upper right chest and lower left side. Ensure no one is touching the patient. Press SHOCK when prompted. Resume CPR immediately after.

Q8: What is the difference between heart attack and cardiac arrest?
A: Heart attack — blocked artery, person is conscious, breathing present. Call emergency and rest. Cardiac arrest — heart stops, person unconscious, no breathing. Start CPR and use AED immediately.

Q9: How do you stop severe bleeding?
A: Press firmly with a clean cloth. Do not lift to check. Add more cloth if soaked through. Elevate the limb. Apply tourniquet 5-7 cm above wound if bleeding does not stop. Write the time it was applied.

Q10: What is shock and how do you treat it?
A: Shock is when organs do not get enough blood. Signs: pale clammy skin, weak fast pulse, confusion. Lay flat, raise legs, keep warm, do not give food or drink, call emergency.

SECTION 3: AIRWAY AND BREATHING EMERGENCIES

Q11: How do you perform the Heimlich maneuver?
A: Stand behind the choking person. Make a fist above the navel below the breastbone. Pull inward and upward firmly. Repeat until object comes out. If they become unconscious, start CPR.

Q12: How do you treat a choking infant?
A: Lay face down on your forearm. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades. Turn face up, give 5 chest thrusts with 2 fingers. Alternate until clear. If unresponsive, start infant CPR.

Q13: How do you manage asthma without an inhaler?
A: Sit upright. Stay calm. Breathe through pursed lips. Try steam with ginger. Drink warm honey-lemon water. Move away from dust or smoke. Call emergency if no improvement in 15 minutes.

Q14: How do you treat a nosebleed?
A: Lean forward. Pinch the soft part of the nose. Hold for 10 minutes. Apply cold compress to the bridge. Breathe through the mouth. Seek help if bleeding continues past 20 minutes.

Q15: What is rescue breathing?
A: Give one breath every 5-6 seconds for adults when person has a pulse but is not breathing. Each breath should make the chest rise. If pulse stops, start full CPR.

SECTION 4: TRAUMA AND INJURIES

Q16: What is RICE treatment?
A: Rest the injured area. Ice wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes every 2 hours. Compression with elastic bandage. Elevation above heart level. Use within first 48 hours.

Q17: How do you splint a broken bone?
A: Do not try to straighten it. Splint it as you find it using a firm board or rolled magazine. Pad around it. Tie above and below the break. Check circulation every 15 minutes.

Q18: How do you treat a dislocated joint?
A: Do not try to put it back. Support with a sling. Apply ice. Do not give food or water. Go to the emergency room for X-ray.

Q19: How do you manage a spinal injury?
A: Do not move the person unless there is immediate danger. Hold head and neck still. Do not remove a helmet. Use jaw thrust if CPR is needed. Wait for emergency services.

Q20: How do you assess a head injury?
A: Use AVPU: Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive. Call emergency if response is below Alert. Watch for unequal pupils, repeated vomiting, fluid from nose or ears, worsening headache, or seizures.

SECTION 5: BURNS AND WOUND CARE

Q21: What are the three degrees of burns?
A: First degree - red, dry, no blisters. Cool with water 10-20 minutes, apply aloe vera. Second degree - blisters and wet. Cool with water, sterile bandage, do not pop blisters. Third degree - white or charred, no pain. Cover loosely and go to hospital immediately, do not cool with water.

Q22: What should you never do for a burn?
A: Never use butter, oil, or toothpaste. Never use ice directly on skin. Never pop blisters. Never remove clothing stuck to a burn. Never put fluffy cotton directly on the burn.

Q23: How do you treat a chemical burn?
A: Brush off dry chemicals first. Flush with running water for 20-30 minutes. Remove contaminated clothing. Do not try to neutralize with acid or base. Cover loosely and go to hospital.

Q24: How do you clean a wound?
A: Wash hands. Rinse wound under clean water for 5-10 minutes. Clean around it with soap. Remove visible debris with clean tweezers. Pat dry. Apply antiseptic. Cover with sterile dressing. Change daily.

Q25: When does a wound need stitches?
A: If deeper than 6 mm, edges cannot stay together, on face or hands or over a joint, bleeding after 15 minutes of pressure, or caused by animal bite or rusty object.

SECTION 6: NEUROLOGICAL EMERGENCIES

Q26: What is the FAST test for stroke?
A: F - Face drooping on one side. A - Arm drifts down when both raised. S - Speech slurred or strange. T - Time to call emergency immediately if any sign is present.

Q27: What is first aid for a seizure?
A: Do not restrain. Do not put anything in the mouth. Clear the area. Cushion the head. Turn to recovery position after convulsions stop. Time it. Call emergency if over 5 minutes.

Q28: What is first aid for a migraine?
A: Rest in a dark quiet room. Apply peppermint oil to temples. Use cold or warm compress on neck or forehead. Drink water. Avoid screens and bright lights.

Q29: How do you help someone having a panic attack?
A: Box breathing: breathe in 4 seconds, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Use 5-4-3-2-1 grounding. Splash cold water on face. Reassure the person they are safe. Loosen tight clothing.

Q30: What is first aid for fainting?
A: Lay flat and raise legs above heart level. Loosen clothing. Give fresh air. Offer small sips of water if conscious. Call emergency if unconscious for more than 1 minute.

SECTION 7: ENVIRONMENTAL EMERGENCIES

Q31: What is the difference between heat exhaustion and heatstroke?
A: Heat exhaustion: cold clammy skin, heavy sweating, alert but weak, below 40 degrees C. Move to cool area, give fluids, rest. Heatstroke: hot skin, may stop sweating, confused or unconscious, above 40 degrees C. Call emergency immediately, cool with wet cloths and ice packs to neck and armpits.

Q32: How do you treat hypothermia?
A: Move to warm shelter. Remove wet clothes. Warm the core first, chest and neck and groin, not hands or feet. Wrap in dry blankets. Give warm sweet drinks if fully conscious. Do not give alcohol. Call emergency for severe cases.

Q33: What is first aid for near drowning?
A: Remove from water safely. Check breathing. Start CPR with 5 rescue breaths if not breathing. Take to hospital even if they seem fine. Risk of secondary drowning within 24 hours.

Q34: How do you treat altitude sickness?
A: Stop going higher. Rest for 24 hours. Drink plenty of water. Take ginger tea for nausea and paracetamol for headache. Descend immediately if symptoms worsen. Call emergency for confusion or pink frothy cough.

Q35: How do you treat frostbite?
A: Move to warm area. Rewarm in warm water 37-40 degrees C, not hot. Do not rub or massage. Do not walk on frostbitten feet. Cover with loose sterile bandage. Seek emergency care.

SECTION 8: POISONING AND TOXIC EMERGENCIES

Q36: What are the four routes of poisoning?
A: Ingested - do not induce vomiting, give small sips of water, call poison control. Inhaled - move to fresh air, give rescue breaths if needed. Absorbed on skin - remove clothes, flush with water 20 or more minutes. Injected - keep still, monitor, go to emergency.

Q37: What is carbon monoxide poisoning?
A: Colorless odorless gas causing headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea. Move everyone outside immediately. Do not re-enter. Call emergency. Start CPR if unconscious and not breathing. Never run engines or generators indoors.

Q38: What is first aid for a snake bite?
A: Keep calm and still. Remove watches and tight clothing near bite. Keep bitten limb below heart level. Do not cut, suck, apply tourniquet, or use ice. Wash gently. Get to hospital with anti-venom within 2-4 hours.

Q39: What is first aid for severe allergic reaction?
A: Signs are throat swelling, difficulty breathing, hives, fast heartbeat, unconsciousness. Use EpiPen on outer thigh if available. Call emergency immediately. Lay flat with legs raised if breathing is fine. Go to hospital even after EpiPen.

Q40: What is first aid for food poisoning?
A: Sip ORS or coconut water. Rest. Avoid solid food briefly then eat bland foods like banana and rice. Avoid dairy and spicy food for 24-48 hours. Seek medical help if blood in stool, high fever, or symptoms last over 48 hours.

SECTION 9: INFECTIOUS DISEASES

Q41: What is diarrhea and how is it treated?
A: Loose watery stools from infection causing dehydration. Give ORS: 1 liter water, 6 teaspoons sugar, half teaspoon salt, lemon juice. Sip slowly. Rest. Avoid heavy meals. Seek medical help if blood in stool or lasting more than 2 days.

Q42: What is malaria and its first aid?
A: Fever and chills from a parasite spread by mosquitoes. Give tulsi or ginger tea for fever. Stay hydrated. Use mosquito nets. Seek medical care immediately for antimalarial drugs.

Q43: What is dengue fever?
A: High fever, severe joint pain, and rash from Aedes mosquito bite. Give coconut water or papaya leaf juice. Rest in cool area. Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen as they worsen bleeding. Go to hospital.

Q44: What is typhoid fever?
A: Sustained high fever and abdominal pain from contaminated water or food. Give boiled water and ORS. Feed soft foods like khichdi. See a doctor for antibiotics.

Q45: What is cholera?
A: Severe watery diarrhea from contaminated water that can kill within hours. Start aggressive ORS immediately. Avoid solid food. Go to emergency care right away.

Q46: What is tuberculosis?
A: Airborne lung infection causing cough lasting 3 or more weeks, night sweats, and weight loss. Eat protein and Vitamin D rich foods. Requires long-course antibiotics from a doctor.

Q47: What is chickenpox?
A: Itchy blisters and fever from a contagious virus. Apply calamine lotion or neem paste. Trim nails to prevent scratching. Drink cool fluids. Keep skin clean.

Q48: What is a urinary tract infection?
A: Burning urination and abdominal pain from bacteria. Drink 8-10 glasses of water daily. Unsweetened cranberry juice may help. Avoid caffeine. See a doctor for antibiotics.

Q49: What is first aid for plant or food poisoning?
A: Do not induce vomiting unless told by poison control. Keep the person awake. Bring a sample of what was eaten to the hospital. Call emergency immediately.

Q50: What is scabies?
A: Intense itching especially at night from mites under skin. Apply neem oil or diluted tea tree oil. Wash all bedding in hot water. See a doctor for permethrin cream.

SECTION 10: METABOLIC AND CHRONIC EMERGENCIES

Q51: What is hypoglycemia?
A: Blood sugar crash causing tremors, sweating, and confusion. If conscious, give jaggery, honey, dates, or glucose drink. Wait 15 minutes. Repeat if needed. Follow with a solid snack. If unconscious, recovery position and call emergency.

Q52: What is hyperglycemia?
A: Excess glucose causing excessive thirst, frequent urination, and blurred vision. Drink water. Gentle walking helps. Avoid sugary drinks. See a doctor.

Q53: What is heatstroke?
A: Body overheats and stops sweating. Signs are confusion and hot dry skin. Move to shade. Sponge with cool water. Apply ice packs to neck and armpits. Give electrolyte drinks. Call emergency.

Q54: What is dehydration?
A: Low body fluid from sweating, vomiting, or diarrhea causing dizziness and dry mouth. Sip ORS or coconut water slowly. Eat water-rich foods. Rest in a cool place.

Q55: What is anemia?
A: Low red blood cells causing fatigue and paleness. Eat iron-rich foods: spinach, lentils, jaggery, dates. Pair with Vitamin C for better absorption. Reduce tea and coffee.

SECTION 11: PEDIATRIC FIRST AID

Q56: How does infant CPR differ from adult CPR?
A: Infant: 2 fingers on center chest, 4 cm deep. Child: 1 or 2 hands, 5 cm deep. Adult: 2 hands, 5-6 cm deep. All use a 30:2 ratio.

Q57: How do you manage a febrile seizure in a child?
A: Stay calm. Clear the area. Do not restrain. Do not put anything in the mouth. Turn on side after seizure ends. Sponge with lukewarm water and give paracetamol. Call emergency if lasts over 5 minutes or child is under 6 months.

Q58: How do you treat a child's high fever?
A: Sponge with lukewarm water. Cool damp cloth on forehead. Dress lightly. Push fluids. Give paracetamol by weight. Seek help if fever exceeds 40 degrees C or lasts more than 3 days.

Q59: How do you treat dehydration in a child?
A: Mild: small sips of ORS. Moderate: continue ORS and see a doctor. Severe with sunken eyes and no urination: emergency care with IV fluids. Never use sports drinks or juice.

Q60: What if a child swallows a small object?
A: If breathing normally do not try to remove it yourself. Go to emergency immediately for X-ray. Watch for drooling or difficulty swallowing. Button batteries and magnets are emergencies.

SECTION 12: EMERGENCY MECHANISMS

Q61: How does CPR pump blood?
A: Chest compression squeezes the heart between the breastbone and spine, forcing blood out. It can provide 25-30% of normal blood flow, enough to keep the brain alive temporarily.

Q62: Why is epinephrine the only treatment for anaphylaxis?
A: Anaphylaxis causes massive histamine release which crashes blood pressure, causes swelling, and closes the airway. Epinephrine is the only substance that reverses all three effects fast enough to save life.

Q63: How does ORS work?
A: Glucose carries sodium across the intestinal wall via the SGLT1 transporter. Water follows. This works even during severe diarrhea when plain water alone cannot rehydrate effectively.

Q64: How does the Heimlich maneuver work?
A: The upward thrust compresses the diaphragm, forces air out of the lungs, and creates pressure up to 300 mmHg in the airway, far more than a normal cough, enough to push out the blockage.

Q65: What is the chain of survival?
A: Recognize and call for help. Start CPR. Use AED early. Paramedic advanced care. Hospital recovery. Each step doubles survival chances. 70% of cardiac arrests happen at home so bystander CPR is critical.

SECTION 13: NATURAL REMEDY MECHANISMS

Q66: How does honey work as a natural treatment?
A: High sugar draws water from bacteria killing them. Enzyme produces hydrogen peroxide as natural antiseptic. Low pH prevents bacterial growth. Anti-inflammatory effect soothes swollen throat. Use raw unprocessed honey.

Q67: How does turmeric reduce inflammation?
A: Curcumin blocks COX-2 enzymes like ibuprofen does. Inhibits NF-kB inflammatory genes. Works best with black pepper which increases absorption by 2000%.

Q68: How does ginger reduce nausea?
A: Gingerols block nausea receptors in the gut. Reduces prostaglandins. Speeds up stomach emptying. Effective for pregnancy nausea, motion sickness, and food poisoning.

Q69: How does steam inhalation work?
A: Warm moist air thins mucus. Dilates nasal blood vessels. Helps cilia sweep mucus out. Adding eucalyptus oil further loosens mucus and mildly opens airways.

Q70: How does cold compress work?
A: Narrows blood vessels to reduce bleeding and swelling. Slows nerve signals to reduce pain. Lowers cell metabolism to protect damaged tissue. Apply 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off. Always wrap ice in cloth.

SECTION 14: COMMON MISTAKES AND PREPAREDNESS

Q71: What are dangerous first aid myths?
A: Never tilt head back for nosebleed, lean forward. Never use butter on burns. Never induce vomiting unless told by poison control. Never remove a helmet after head injury. Never suck snake venom. Never give liquids to an unconscious person. Never rub frostbitten skin. Never put anything in a seizure victim's mouth. Scrape bee stinger sideways, never squeeze.

Q72: When should you not move an injured person?
A: Do not move if you suspect head, neck, or spine injury, if moving would worsen a fracture, if they are impaled, or if they are in cardiac arrest. Exception: move immediately if there is fire, flooding, or toxic gas.

Q73: What emergency numbers should everyone know in Nepal?
A: Ambulance: 102. Fire: 101. Police: 100. Nepal Telecom helpline: 198. Memorize the nearest hospital address.

Q74: What should a first aid kit contain?
A: Sterile gauze, adhesive bandages, elastic bandage, triangular bandage, medical tape, antiseptic wipes, betadine, blunt scissors, tweezers, gloves, face shield, thermometer, paracetamol, antihistamine, ORS sachets, eye wash, emergency contacts list.

Q75: What is triage?
A: Sorting multiple victims by urgency. Red: life-threatening but survivable, treat first. Yellow: serious but stable, can wait. Green: minor injuries, treat last. Black: unsurvivable or dead. Assess each victim in under 60 seconds.
