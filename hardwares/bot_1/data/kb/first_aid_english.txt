Q: What's diarrhea, and what should I do first?
A: Diarrhea is an intestinal infection from bacteria/viruses that messes up water absorption in your gut, causing loose stools, cramps, and dehydration. It works by inflaming the gut lining, forcing out fluids rapidly. If untreated, it leads to severe weakness, organ failure, or even death from fluid loss. 

Natural first aid: Mix a homemade ORS (1 liter water + 6 tsp sugar + ½ tsp salt + lemon juice), sip slowly, rest, and skip heavy meals.

Q: Tell me about malaria.
A: Malaria comes from Plasmodium parasites spread by mosquitoes, infecting your red blood cells and causing chills and cyclic fevers. The parasite multiplies in your liver then bursts blood cells, releasing toxins. Untreated, it causes anemia, brain damage, or fatal organ failure. 

Natural first aid: Brew tulsi or ginger tea to ease fever, stay super hydrated, and use nets to block mosquitoes.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How does dengue fever work?
A: It's a virus from Aedes mosquitoes causing high fever, killer joint pain, and rashes. The virus damages blood vessels and drops platelet counts, leading to leaks. Without treatment, you risk internal bleeding, shock, or death. 

Natural first aid: Drink coconut water or fresh papaya leaf juice to boost hydration and platelets; rest in a cool spot.

Q: Explain an asthma attack.
A: Asthma is chronic airway inflammation that flares with triggers like dust, narrowing your breathing tubes. Immune chemicals swell the bronchi, blocking air. If ignored, it can cause respiratory failure or lasting lung harm. 

Natural first aid: Breathe steam from hot water with ginger slices, sit upright, and sip warm honey-lemon water.

Q: What's the deal with the flu (influenza)?
A: A respiratory virus that invades nose, throat, and lung cells, replicating fast to cause fever, cough, and aches. Untreated, it can spark pneumonia, especially in kids or elderly. 

Natural first aid: Make a ginger-garlic-tulsi decoction, drink lots of fluids, and get plenty of rest.

Q: What is hypoglycemia (low blood sugar)?
A: Common in diabetics, it's a sudden blood glucose crash from too much insulin or missed meals, starving your brain and body. It triggers tremors and confusion. Untreated: seizures, coma, or brain damage. 

Natural first aid: If conscious, give jaggery, dates, or honey; then rest and monitor.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How does heatstroke happen?
A: Your body overheats from heat or overexertion, failing to sweat and cool down, which cooks your proteins. Symptoms: confusion, hot dry skin. If untreated: brain swelling, organ shutdown, death. 

Natural first aid: Get to shade, sponge with cool water or ice packs on neck/armpits, sip electrolyte drinks.

Q: Describe anaphylaxis.

Q: What should I do if someone is choking?
A: Choking happens when an object blocks the airway. If they can cough, encourage it. If they can't breathe/speak, perform the Heimlich maneuver (abdominal thrusts). Stand behind them, wrap arms around waist, make a fist above navel, and pull inward/upward.

Natural first aid: Ensure airway is clear once object is out; sip warm water to soothe throat.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How to recognize a heart attack and what to do?
A: Signs include crushing chest pain, pain in left arm/jaw, and shortness of breath. It's a blockage of blood flow to the heart muscle. Untreated: permanent heart damage or death.

Natural first aid: Have the person sit and rest to reduce heart load. If they have prescribed aspirin, they can chew it (under medical advice). Call emergency services immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: What is the first aid for a snake bite?
A: Snake venom can affect the nervous system or blood. Keep the victim calm and still to slow venom spread. Do NOT cut the wound or try to suck out venom. 

Natural first aid: Wash the area with soap/water, keep the bitten limb below heart level, and get to a hospital with anti-venom immediately.

────────────────────────────────────

Q: How to handle a possible bone fracture?
A: Symptoms include deformity, inability to move the limb, and intense pain. Untreated: improper healing or nerve damage.

Natural first aid: Immobilize the area using a splint (stick/board) and bandage. Apply ice to reduce swelling (not directly on skin). Do not try to realign the bone.

Emergency and Trauma
════════════════════════════════════
Q: What should I do for severe bleeding?
 A: Severe bleeding happens when a blood vessel is damaged, causing rapid blood loss. The body tries to form clots, but heavy bleeding can overwhelm this process. If untreated, it may lead to shock, organ failure, or death.
Natural first aid: Apply firm pressure with a clean cloth, elevate the injured area if possible, and keep the person lying down. Do not remove blood-soaked dressings; place new ones on top.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is a concussion?
 A: A concussion is a mild brain injury caused by a blow or sudden jolt to the head. The brain temporarily loses normal function, leading to headache, dizziness, confusion, or memory problems. If untreated, repeated injuries can cause serious brain damage.
Natural first aid: Let the person rest in a quiet place, avoid strenuous activity, and monitor for worsening symptoms such as vomiting or loss of consciousness.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: How should I handle a nosebleed?
 A: A nosebleed occurs when small blood vessels inside the nose rupture due to injury, dry air, or irritation. Most are harmless, but heavy bleeding can cause significant blood loss.
Natural first aid: Sit upright, lean slightly forward, and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes. Apply a cool cloth to the bridge of the nose.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What happens during fainting (syncope)?
 A: Fainting occurs when blood flow to the brain temporarily drops, causing a brief loss of consciousness. Common triggers include dehydration, stress, pain, or standing for long periods. If untreated, injuries from falls may occur.
Natural first aid: Lay the person flat, raise their legs slightly, loosen tight clothing, and allow fresh air. Offer water once they are fully awake.

Respiratory Issues
════════════════════════════════════
Q: What is pneumonia?
 A: Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in the lungs, causing them to fill with fluid or pus. This makes breathing difficult and reduces oxygen delivery to the body. Untreated cases can lead to respiratory failure.
Natural first aid: Encourage rest, warm fluids, and adequate hydration. Keep the person in a comfortable upright position to ease breathing.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is hyperventilation?
 A: Hyperventilation occurs when a person breathes too quickly, causing carbon dioxide levels in the blood to fall. This can result in dizziness, tingling sensations, and chest tightness. Severe cases may cause fainting.
Natural first aid: Help the person sit comfortably, encourage slow controlled breathing, and reassure them while reducing stressors around them.

Q: What is food poisoning?
 A: Food poisoning occurs when contaminated food or drink introduces harmful bacteria, viruses, or toxins into the digestive system. It often causes vomiting, diarrhea, stomach cramps, and dehydration. Severe dehydration can become life-threatening.
Natural first aid: Drink ORS or clean fluids frequently, eat light foods such as rice or bananas, and avoid oily or spicy meals until recovery.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is typhoid fever?
 A: Typhoid is a bacterial infection spread through contaminated food and water. The bacteria enter the bloodstream and cause prolonged fever, weakness, stomach pain, and digestive problems. Untreated cases can damage the intestines and other organs.
Natural first aid: Encourage rest, provide plenty of clean fluids, and maintain good hygiene while seeking medical evaluation.

Q: What is dehydration?
 A: Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluids than it takes in. This reduces blood volume and affects normal organ function. Symptoms include thirst, dizziness, weakness, and dark urine. Severe dehydration can lead to shock.
Natural first aid: Sip water, ORS, coconut water, or other electrolyte-rich fluids frequently. Rest in a cool environment and avoid heavy activity.

Q: What are muscle cramps?
 A: Muscle cramps are sudden, painful contractions often caused by dehydration, overuse, or electrolyte imbalance. The affected muscle tightens involuntarily and becomes painful. Untreated severe cramps can limit movement and increase injury risk.
Natural first aid: Gently stretch and massage the muscle, drink water with electrolytes, and rest until the pain subsides.

Q: What is a sprain?
 A: A sprain occurs when the ligaments around a joint are stretched or torn, usually due to twisting or sudden movement. Common symptoms include pain, swelling, bruising, and difficulty moving the joint. If untreated, the joint may become unstable or weak.
Natural first aid: Follow the RICE method — Rest the injured area, apply Ice for 15–20 minutes, use Compression with a bandage, and keep the limb Elevated above heart level.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What should I do if something gets into the eye?
 A: Dust, sand, insects, or other small particles can irritate the eye and cause redness, watering, and discomfort. Rubbing the eye can scratch the cornea and worsen the injury.
Natural first aid: Rinse the eye gently with clean water. Avoid rubbing the eye. If the object does not come out or vision becomes blurry, seek medical help.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What happens during an electric shock?
 A: Electric shock occurs when electrical current passes through the body, affecting nerves, muscles, and the heart. Severe shocks can cause burns, cardiac arrest, or unconsciousness.
Natural first aid: Turn off the power source before touching the person. Use a non-conductive object such as wood or plastic to move them away from the source. Check breathing and pulse, and seek emergency medical care.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is the first aid for an animal bite?
 A: Animal bites can damage skin and tissues while introducing bacteria or viruses into the body. Some bites may transmit rabies or serious infections. If untreated, complications can become life-threatening.
Natural first aid: Wash the wound thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes. Cover with a clean dressing and seek medical attention immediately.

 Temperature-Related Conditions
 ════════════════════════════════════
Q: What is hypothermia?
 A: Hypothermia occurs when the body's core temperature drops dangerously low due to prolonged exposure to cold conditions. It slows down vital body functions, causing confusion, shivering, and weakness. Untreated hypothermia can lead to organ failure and death.
Natural first aid: Move the person to a warm place, remove wet clothing, wrap them in blankets, and provide warm drinks if they are fully conscious.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is sunburn?
 A: Sunburn is skin damage caused by excessive exposure to ultraviolet (UV) rays from the sun. Symptoms include redness, pain, swelling, and sometimes blisters. Severe sunburn can lead to dehydration and skin infection.
Natural first aid: Cool the affected skin with cold water or a damp cloth, drink plenty of fluids, and apply fresh aloe vera gel.

 A: Poisoning occurs when harmful chemicals, medications, or toxic substances enter the body. Symptoms may include vomiting, stomach pain, confusion, difficulty breathing, or unconsciousness. Severe poisoning can cause organ failure.

 Neurological Emergencies
 ════════════════════════════════════
Q: What should I do if someone has a seizure?
 A: A seizure is caused by sudden abnormal electrical activity in the brain. It can result in uncontrolled body movements, loss of consciousness, or unusual behavior. Most seizures stop within a few minutes.
Natural first aid: Move dangerous objects away from the person, place something soft under their head, and turn them onto their side once the seizure stops. Do not restrain them or put anything in their mouth.
────────────────────────────────────
Q: What is a stroke and how can I recognize it?
 A: A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked or a blood vessel bursts. Symptoms include facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulties, and sudden confusion. Untreated strokes can cause permanent brain damage or death.
Natural first aid: Call emergency services immediately. Keep the person comfortable and monitor their breathing. Do not give food, drink, or medication unless directed by a healthcare professional.
